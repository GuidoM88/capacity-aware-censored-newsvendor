# Publication assessment and a sharp exclusion bound

November 1, 2026. The strongest current theoretical candidate is an exact answer to a worst-case question. For a fixed positive cost split, how much can allowing partial participation exaggerate the largest saving available to a real coalition? Both the number of exclusions and the shares matter. The expected cost of the implemented offer sets the scale.

## Where prior results apply

* Chen and Zhang's inventory centralization game already includes a two-scenario newsvendor instance with symmetric penalties. Their Theorem 7 proves that deciding whether a given allocation admits a blocking coalition is NP-hard, using equal charges. Their Example 1 also distinguishes the ordinary core from the allocations generated by optimal grand-coalition dual prices. The allocation in their hardness reduction can be implemented by a forecast in our model. Keep the same distribution of total output $T$ and set each forecast component to $T/n$. The forecast's expected dual charges then split its optimal cost equally, and the realized grand cost is the same because the total law is correct. Thus the general hardness claim, even with that forecast restriction, is already accounted for. We inspected the example and the reduction in the published paper. [Chen and Zhang (2009), Example 1 and §5](https://publish.illinois.edu/xinchen/files/2012/12/SPinventorygame.pdf)
* Aubin formalized fractional participation in cooperative games. His core-equivalence statement concerns a particular canonical fuzzy extension. Our extension assigns a physical balancing cost to the weighted production $z^\top Y$. Denault gives the corresponding full-box cost inequalities and their connection to the subdifferential for homogeneous costs. Our hierarchy tests restricted faces and measures a positive violation for a frozen allocation. This establishes a direct precedent for the underlying distinction between integer and fractional participants. The inspected sources do not state the sharp depth-and-share formula below. [Aubin (1981), publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.6.1.1) · [Denault (2001), §5, Eq. 7 and Theorems 6–7](https://www.ressources-actuarielles.net/EXT/ISFA/1226.nsf/0/e0678cad2d06cd0bc1257bd20041fcd1/%24FILE/CoherentAllocation.pdf)
* The proposed paired block statistic is a direct specialization of Mak, Morton and Wood. Use the negative of our face objective as their minimization objective and the frozen feasible coalition and offer as their candidate. In one block their candidate value minus sample optimum becomes $D_1(\widehat P)-e_*(\widehat P)$. Their Theorem 1 supplies the expectation inequality, and §3.2 pairs the evaluations on common samples. The remaining step $e_*(P)\le B(P)$ is feasibility. A bounded finite-sample concentration interval would change the calibration of this standard estimator. The general block procedure belongs in the comparison. [Mak, Morton and Wood (1999), §§2.2 and 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-6377(98)00054-6) · [Institutional copy](https://calhoun.nps.edu/bitstream/10945/38421/1/MakMortonWood.pdf)

## Maximum possible overstatement at each depth

Use the population model in the [exclusion note](exclusion.md). Let $L>0$ be the **expected** imbalance cost of the implemented grand offer, let $w_i>0$ sum to one and let $a_i=Lw_i$. Write $B$ for the largest positive saving among actual proper coalitions. Level $D_k$ checks the integer coalitions omitting fewer than $k$ producers and optimizes fractional memberships over each face omitting $k$. Let $s_k(w)$ denote the sum of the $n-k$ largest shares.

**Theorem.** For every $n\ge3$, every such $w$ and $1\le k\le n-2$,

$$
0\le D_k-B\le [L s_k(w)-B]_+,\qquad
\sup\frac{D_k-B}{L}=s_k(w).
$$

The supremum is over bounded instances with the specified shares. It is the same if all producers have unit capacity and nonzero variance, there are two equiprobable states, the loss is symmetric, the forecast gives the correct distribution of total output, and every actual proper coalition is strictly stable. At $k=n-1$ the gap is zero. With equal shares the nonterminal supremum is $(n-k)/n$.

**Upper bound.** Every fractional member vector on a depth-$k$ face has assigned charges at most $Ls_k(w)$, and balancing costs are nonnegative. Every directly checked integer coalition has excess at most $B$. Taking their maximum gives the stated bound. The terminal-level identity follows from positive homogeneity on a one-coordinate face.

**Construction.** Relabel the two largest-share producers as the first and last, breaking ties arbitrarily. Fix $m\ge2$ and put $H=m+2(n-2)$. Before a common rescaling, the two production vectors are

$$
Y^{\rm hi}=(m,2,\ldots,2,0),\qquad
Y^{\rm lo}=(0,0,\ldots,0,H-1).
$$

Use unit absolute loss. The two totals are $H$ and $H-1$. In the forecast, give producer $j$ output $w_jT$ in each state of total $T$. The midpoint offer is optimal under both laws. The forecast dual score is $+1$ in the high state and $-1$ in the low state. It assigns $w_j/2$ to producer $j$, so the forecast induces exactly the prescribed weights and $L=1/2$. Dividing all outputs and offers by $H$ gives unit physical bounds, positive producer variances, and $L=1/(2H)$ without changing any excess-to-$L$ ratios.

With $d=Y^{\rm hi}-Y^{\rm lo}$ in the unscaled units, the optimized cost for any vector of memberships $z$ is $|d^\top z|/2$. An integer coalition excluding the last producer has a positive state difference of at least two. A proper coalition including the last producer omits positive differences of total at least two, leaving a difference at most $-1$. Consequently every nonempty proper coalition costs at least $L$, strictly more than its own charge, and $B=0$.

Omit the $k$ smallest shares, all among the middle producers. Include every remaining positive-difference producer fully. Give the last producer participation

$$
r=\frac{H-2k}{H-1}=1-\frac{2k-1}{H-1}.
$$

This membership has zero state difference and zero balancing cost. Its charge divided by $L$ is

$$
s_k(w)-w_n\frac{2k-1}{H-1}.
$$

Letting $m$ grow proves the matching supremum, for the same sequence of instances at every nonterminal depth. In fact the last display is the exact ratio for each finite member of this family. On a face containing the last producer, for any choices of the other memberships the best last participation cancels the state difference. It lies in $[0,1]$ because the face omits at least one positive producer. The slopes of the normalized objective in that last coordinate are $w_n+(H-1)>0$ and $w_n-(H-1)<0$. Once balanced, increasing any free positive membership increases the objective. The best omissions are therefore $k$ of the middle producers with smallest shares. A face omitting the last producer has maximum zero. This verifies the formula used for the numerical check. QED.

The absolute-sum form of the two-state cost is also the device in Chen and Zhang's reduction. The additional step here is matching the elementary upper bound for **each depth and prescribed positive split**, while generating the split from a forecast with the correct aggregate law. This is a short extremal calculation. Establishing priority for the exact statement requires further independent comparison.

## What additional information can improve the bound

For the general population game write $c_j=c_P(\{j\})$ and set

$$
\rho=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\{a_j/c_j:c_j>0\}\right).
$$

Then the same hierarchy satisfies

$$
D_k-B\le(1-\rho)[Ls_k(w)-B]_+ .
$$

**Proof.** On a face $F$ put $x=\sum_{j\in F}a_j(1-z_j)$. Nonnegative cost gives $a^\top z-c_P(z)-B\le a(F)-B-x$. By subadditivity and homogeneity of optimized balancing cost,
$c_P(F)\le c_P(z)+\sum_{j\in F}(1-z_j)c_j$. The whole face is an actual proper coalition, so $B\ge a(F)-c_P(F)$. Together these inequalities bound the same excess over $B$ by $(1/\rho-1)x$. The minimum of the two bounds is at most their weighted average with weights $1-\rho$ and $\rho$, which cancels $x$. Take the maximum over faces and the directly checked integer coalitions. QED.

The ratio measures the smallest share of a producer's standalone cost covered by its assigned charge. For example, if every producer pays at least half its standalone cost, this bound halves the share-only ceiling. This is a population corollary based on subadditivity. The [November 4 finite-data note](standalone_certificate.md) answers the next question of how tightly a validation sample can certify the ratio on the development grid. If rho=0, use the share-only bound directly; if L=0, every positive excess and relaxation value is zero.

## Checks, scale and publication judgment

Exact coalition enumeration and the existing face LP agree with the analytical values for $n=3,6,8$, three values of $m$, equal and unequal positive shares, and every depth. The 18 laws give 84 LP comparisons, including terminal levels. With six equal-share producers, $k=2$ and $m=200$, the model reports a possible saving of about 66.4% of actual expected grand cost, while every actual coalition is strictly stable. Run the following command to regenerate the [saved results](../results/exclusion/sharp_gap.json).

    python scripts/check_exclusion_ceiling.py

The experimental screening threshold in the [finite-data study](face_residuals.md) used $A$, the **maximum possible** loss of the frozen offer. In the rescaled construction $L=1/(2H)$ while $A$ is bounded away from zero. Thus $L/A$ tends to zero and the extremal construction has a vanishing gap relative to $A$. The formula answers the expected-cost question. Its near-worst-case behavior does not establish that an operational certificate misses the previous 5%-of-$A$ target. The examples specify only aggregate calibration, permitting individual forecast errors.

**Publication judgment.** The sharp formula is a defensible candidate for a theoretical component of a paper. The broad fractional-membership distinction, the general hardness claim, and the paired block procedure all have direct precedents. The exact formula was not found in the inspected papers. Its short derivation from familiar two-state geometry and its scale limitation leave its standalone significance open. Submission would be premature on this evidence. The next discriminating result would identify from finite data when the fractional overstatement is small, with a meaningful accuracy or computation advantage against exhaustive checking and the standard paired-gap baseline. The earlier 1/48 precision result provides the benchmark for that claim.

## Finite-data follow-up, November 4

The standalone condition now has a proved iid certificate. An intersection-union
test followed by a fixed increasing sequence avoids unnecessary producer/grid
multiplicity penalties for the single global ratio. These are established
statistical principles, explicitly credited in the proof. An independent
pilot selects offers and one feasible coalition witness. Validation yields
simultaneous bounds for the relaxation gap at all depths.

The [prespecified comparison](../results/standalone/summary.md) uses 96 original
positive-share laws, 12 exchangeable controls, six methods and three sample sizes.
At 4,096 pilot plus 4,096 validation observations the conditional screen passes:
35/35 original laws with rho >= 0.6 certify rho >= 0.5. The matched Bonferroni
and full-budget DKW comparisons also pass 35/35. The broad precision target
fails, with 5/47 heterogeneous laws certifying a gap below 5% of maximum loss;
the face baseline passes 0/47 on these same fresh draws. Before drawing them,
the population calculation already limited the corollary to 8/47.

Combining paired and face bounds improves the heterogeneous mean normalized
gap from 0.143603 for the face method to 0.128273. A matched DKW-plus-face
combination gives 0.129887, so the candidate's advantage over this alternative
is only about 1.24% in that mean. These additional combinations are labeled
post-run diagnostics. The standard SAA block comparison was also implemented;
its fixed block size does not pass the small-gap screen. This does not settle
the block-size/concentration tradeoff.

**Updated judgment.** This cycle adds a verifiable sufficient condition and a
measured scope to the theoretical result. It does not establish a generally
superior statistical algorithm. Preserve it as supporting material and focus
the next priority check on the exact depth/share theorem and its refinement.
Their originality and sufficient theoretical significance remain unresolved;
the finite-data result neither certifies publication readiness nor disproves
that the sharp theorem could support a paper.
