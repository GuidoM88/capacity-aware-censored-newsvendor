# A seminorm oracle for structural exclusion envelopes

November 6, 2026, branch `pulp-fiction`. This note adds a computational tool
for the structural-parameter question of [C39](three_player_rho.md) and
records what it shows. It proves several upper bounds and maps where they
are attained by a relaxation. It does **not** check whether demand laws
realize the relaxed values, except where C39 already does. It is not a claim
of a general theorem, bibliographic priority or publication significance.

Use the notation of the manuscript: depth-one bound D_1, integer excess B,
expected grand cost L of the implemented offer, charges a=Lw and
standalone costs c_j. Normalize L=1. Throughout, the loss is symmetric
absolute loss, so the optimized cost extends to a seminorm on signed
memberships (as used in the C39 proof). The standalone ratio condition
rho >= r means c_j <= a_j/r.

## 1. The oracle: smallest cost compatible with the game

Let 0<r<=1 and B>=0. Let C be the set of seminorms c on R^n with

$$
c(e_j)\le a_j/r,\qquad c(\mathbf 1)\le 1,\qquad
c(\mathbf 1_S)\ge \ell_S:=a(S)-B\quad(\emptyset\ne S\subsetneq N).
$$

Optionally some singleton lower bounds are raised, for example to a_j/r
(Section 5). Every demand law with these parameters gives an element of C,
because L is at least the optimized grand cost.

**Lemma 1 (gauge duality).** For every z,

$$
\min_{c\in C}c(z)=\max_{S}\ \min\{|\pi^\top z|:\pi\in\Pi_S\},
\qquad
\Pi_S=\{\pi:\ |\pi_j|\le a_j/r,\ |\mathbf 1^\top\pi|\le1,\ \pi^\top\mathbf 1_S\ge\ell_S\},
$$

with the convention that the value is +infinity when some Pi_S is empty.
The minimum is attained.

*Proof.* For v>=0 let gamma_v be the gauge generated by the e_j with
weights a_j/r, the vector 1 with weight 1, and z with weight v:

$$
\gamma_v(x)=\min\Bigl\{\sum_j|\lambda_j|\tfrac{a_j}{r}+|\mu|+|s|v:\
x=\sum_j\lambda_je_j+\mu\mathbf 1+sz\Bigr\}.
$$

It is a finite seminorm because the e_j span R^n. Any seminorm c obeying
the three upper bounds c(e_j)<=a_j/r, c(1)<=1, c(z)<=v satisfies c<=gamma_v
by the triangle inequality and homogeneity applied to each decomposition;
gamma_v itself obeys them. Hence some c in C has c(z)<=v if and only if
gamma_v satisfies every lower bound. LP duality gives

$$
\gamma_v(\mathbf 1_S)=\max\{\pi^\top\mathbf 1_S:\ |\pi_j|\le a_j/r,\
|\mathbf 1^\top\pi|\le1,\ |\pi^\top z|\le v\},
$$

so gamma_v(1_S)>=l_S exactly when some pi in Pi_S has |pi'z|<=v.
The smallest admissible v is the displayed max-min, and gamma at that v is
an element of C attaining it. QED.

For a fixed membership this is one small LP per proper coalition. The
relaxed depth-one envelope maximizes a'z-B-min_C c(z) over the one-exclusion
faces. By homogeneity a positive maximizer can be scaled until one free
coordinate equals one. The implementation is
`censored_newsvendor.seminorm_envelope`; the maximization over z is a grid
search with local refinement. Consequently:

* the relaxed value is an **upper bound** on the worst gap over demand laws;
* a found value equal to a proved upper bound **certifies** the relaxation's
  value;
* a found value strictly below every proved bound is a **numerical
  observation**: a finer search could find more.

The [saved map](../results/exclusion/seminorm_envelope.md) reproduces C39
at all ten tested ratios, including C30's value 2/3 at r=0 (where the
singleton caps are dropped).

## 2. Equal shares, any n: the separable bound

**Proposition 2.** For n>=3 equal shares and 0<r<=1,

$$
\frac{D_1-B}{L}\le U_n(r)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(1-r)(n-1)}{n}, & r\le\dfrac{n-2}{n},\\[6pt]
\dfrac{(n-2)(1-r^2)}{2nr}, & r\ge\dfrac{n-2}{n}.
\end{cases}
$$

For n=3 this is the C39 upper bound. For r<=(n-2)/n it equals the depth-one
C31 ceiling, so this argument improves C31 only above (n-2)/n.

*Proof.* Put a=1/n and M=a/r. Take a face F with |F|=n-1 and a
membership z on it with f(z)>B, scaled so that a unit coordinate u has
z_u=1. For any S subset of F, the actual coalition S satisfies
c(1_S)>=a|S|-B, and by the triangle inequality and symmetry

$$
c(\mathbf 1_S)\le c(z)+\sum_{j\in S}(1-z_j)c_j+\sum_{j\in F\setminus S}z_jc_j .
$$

With c_j<=M this gives
f(z)-B <= sum over S of (1-z_j)(M-a) plus sum over F\S of z_j(M+a).
Choosing S coordinatewise, f(z)-B <= sum_j h(z_j) with
h(t)=min{(1-t)(M-a), t(M+a)} and h(1)=0. Nonnegative cost also gives
f(z)-B <= a sum_j z_j. The minimum of these two bounds is concave and
symmetric in the n-2 coordinates other than u, so averaging over their
permutations shows that a common value t is optimal:

$$
\max_{t\in[0,1]}\min\{a(1+(n-2)t),\ (n-2)h(t)\}.
$$

The function h peaks at t=(1-r)/2 with value a(1-r^2)/(2r). If
r>=(n-2)/n the first term is not smaller there, giving the second line.
Otherwise the maximum is where a(1+(n-2)t) meets (n-2)(1-t)(M-a), at
t=(1-r)-r/(n-2), with value a(n-1)(1-r). QED.

**Numerical observation (n=4,5).** In the saved map the relaxation attains
U_n in 11 of 16 equal-share cases. The five exceptions lie in windows below
(n-2)/n. For n=4 the best values found at r=0.22, 0.25 and 0.3 are strictly
below C31, e.g. 31/56=0.553571 at r=1/4 against 9/16=0.5625, at the
membership (1, 6/7, 5/14, 0) whose cost is zero. The C31 value is attained
for r<=1/5 and on [1/3,1/2], and U_4 above 1/2. For n=5 the samples
r=0.35 and 0.4 lie below C31 (0.511429 and 0.46 against 0.52 and 0.48),
while r=0.25 and 0.5 attain it. Section 4 gives a heuristic that predicted
the n=5 window from n=4.

## 3. Three players with arbitrary shares: a fourth inequality

**Proposition 3.** For three players, positive shares a and 0<r<=1,

$$
\frac{D_1-B}{L}\le\max_{i\ne j}\ \max_{0\le t\le1}\min\Bigl\{
a_i+ta_j,\ ta_j\bigl(1+\tfrac1r\bigr),\ (1-t)a_j\bigl(\tfrac1r-1\bigr),\
(1-t)a_i\bigl(1+\tfrac1r\bigr)\Bigr\},
$$

the outer maximum over ordered pairs (i integral, j fractional).

*Proof.* As in C39, a positive maximizer scales to z=e_i+te_j on the face
{i,j}. The first three terms are the C39 inequalities with c_j<=a_j/r:
nonnegative cost; c_i<=c(z)+tc_j with c_i>=a_i-B; and
c_ij<=c(z)+(1-t)c_j with c_ij>=a_i+a_j-B. For the fourth, te_j=z-e_i gives
t c_j <= c(z)+c_i <= c(z)+a_i/r, while the pair inequality gives
c_j >= (a_i+a_j-B-c(z))/(1-t) for t<1. Eliminating c_j,

$$
c(z)\ge t(a_i+a_j-B)-(1-t)\frac{a_i}{r},
\qquad
f(z)-B\le(1-t)\Bigl(a_i+\frac{a_i}{r}\Bigr)-(1-t)B .
$$

At t=1 the membership is an actual coalition and f(z)-B<=0. QED.

With equal shares the fourth term is never below the third, so the bound
is C39. With unequal shares it matters. **Negative result:** the C39
argument applied verbatim is not sharp. For w=(0.6,0.25,0.15) and r=0.2 it
gives 0.68, while the relaxation's value is 0.607143, which is the
four-inequality bound, attained at z=e_2+(25/42)e_1.

In the saved map (four share vectors, six ratios) the relaxation attains the
four-inequality bound in all 24 cases. The three-inequality bound is
strictly loose in 7 of them, by up to 0.151579 (w=(0.7,0.2,0.1), r=0.2:
0.568421 against 0.72). This suggests that Proposition 3 is the exact
relaxed envelope for three players. Whether demand laws attain it is the
open part of a three-player extension of C39.

## 4. A heuristic for the windows (not a theorem)

For n equal shares, consider memberships with p unit coordinates and
q=n-1-p fractional coordinates at a common value t. Reaching the C31 value
a(n-1)(1-r) with the bounds of Proposition 2 forces zero cost at z, tight
standalone caps on the fractional coordinates and additivity of c on them.
The low-regime intersection exists when r<=q/(q+2p). The caps of the unit
coordinates then require tq<=p, i.e. r>=(q-p)/(q+p). The heuristic predicts
C31 where some p satisfies both inequalities, U_n above (n-2)/n, and a value
below C31 elsewhere. It gives the n=4 window (1/5,1/3) and predicted the n=5
window (1/3,1/2) before that run. These are necessary conditions for one
family of witnesses; other memberships could attain C31.

The intervals are closed. A first version used half-open intervals and
mislabeled r=1/5 for n=4, where the relaxation attains C31: at an endpoint
the window has zero depth. With closed intervals the labels agree with the
attained/not-attained pattern in all 16 saved equal-share cases.

If the heuristic is right in general, consecutive windows exist only while
p^2+p+1<n, each has width of order 2/n, and they lie near the top of
[0,(n-2)/n]. The improvement over C31 would then survive mainly for
r>=(n-2)/n. This is the main risk for a paper built on the rho envelope
alone: for larger n it may largely reproduce the elementary C31 ceiling.

## 5. Charges proportional to standalone costs

If charges are proportional to standalone costs, a_j/c_j is the same for
every producer, so rho=L/(c_1+...+c_n); with an optimal grand offer this
is the pooling ratio c(N)/sum_j c_j. The simplest case pins every
singleton cost.

**Proposition 4.** Let n=3, equal shares a=1/3, L=1, and c_j=M=a/r for
every j. Then (D_1-B)/L <= P(r), where

$$
P(r)=\begin{cases}
0, & r\le r_0=\dfrac{2\sqrt3-3}{3}\approx0.1547,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1+r}{2}-\dfrac{1-3r}{6r}, & r_0\le r\le\dfrac15,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1-r}{3}, & r\ge\dfrac15.
\end{cases}
$$

*Proof.* Take z=e_i+te_j. Besides nonnegative cost: c_i=M<=c(z)+tM;
c_ij>=2a-B with c_ij<=c(z)+(1-t)M; and, from the grand coalition,
c_ij=c(1-e_k)>=c_k-c(1)>=M-1, again with c_ij<=c(z)+(1-t)M. These give

$$
f(z)-B\le\min\{a(1+t),\ a(1+t)-M(1-t),\ a(1+t)-2a+(1-t)M,\ a(1+t)-tM+1\}.
$$

The second and third lines meet at t=1-r with value a(1-r); the fourth is
not lower there iff r>=1/5. Otherwise the second and fourth meet at
t=(M+1)/(2M) with the middle expression, positive iff 3r^2+6r-1>0. QED.

The relaxation attains P(r) at all 13 tested ratios (saved map). Compared
with C39, which allows arbitrary standalone costs, proportional charges
remove the whole gap under strong pooling, and for rho>=1/5 halve the C31
ceiling. The worst case, 4/15 of L, is at intermediate pooling rho=1/5.
Whether demand laws realize P(r) is open.

## What this does not establish

* Realizability: apart from C39 (three equal shares), no demand law is
  constructed for the relaxed values above.
* The n=4 and n=5 windows below C31 are not certified; they are the best
  values found by the search.
* Positive integer excess was only sampled: in the ten sampled cases with
  B>0 the relaxed gap is never larger than at B=0.
* No general-n theorem, no performance claim, and no priority or
  significance claim follows. C7 remains open.

## Suggested next steps

1. **Realizability tests.** Search finite centrally symmetric laws (sums of
   independent signs, as in C39) for: n=4 at r=0.15 (C31), r=0.25 (window)
   and r=0.6 (high regime); three players with w=(0.6,0.25,0.15), r=0.2;
   the proportional case at r=0.17 and r=0.3. If they attain the relaxed
   values, Lemma 1 plus explicit constructions is a viable proof route for
   a general statement. If not, realizability is the mathematical core.
2. **Certify one window** (n=4, r=1/4) exactly, using the LP dual
   certificates of Lemma 1 on a finite candidate set and a matching argument.
3. **Choose the central question.** The proportional-charge envelope as a
   function of the pooling ratio is economically interpretable and already
   shows a non-monotone shape at n=3. The rho-floor envelope with arbitrary
   charges risks reproducing C31 for larger n (Section 4).
